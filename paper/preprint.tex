\begin{abstract}
We study a small decision-making agent for diagnosing integration-test failures in continuous integration (CI). The agent treats the true failure cause as hidden, maintains competing beliefs, selects diagnostic actions, updates beliefs after observations, and escalates when evidence is insufficient or intervention is required. The prototype compares a fixed baseline (P0), a threshold policy (P2), and an information-value policy (P3) in a reproducible simulated environment. Across five fixed seeds and 120 cases per seed, P3 reduced diagnostic effort relative to P0 while retaining competitive accuracy, but it did not universally dominate P2 across commitment thresholds. The results are simulation evidence under explicit assumptions, not estimates of real-world CI failure frequencies.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}
CI failures can have multiple plausible causes: code regression, flaky tests, external dependencies, CI infrastructure, test-data/state, or an unknown/other category. A useful diagnostic agent should choose the next information-gathering action rather than immediately asserting a root cause. The research framing distinguishes observations from hidden causes and emphasizes traceable belief updates and human escalation.

\section{Research framing}
The prototype follows the loop
\[
\text{failure} \rightarrow \text{evidence} \rightarrow \text{belief update} \rightarrow \text{diagnostic action} \rightarrow \text{feedback}.
\]
Candidate actions include rerunning a test, searching history, checking a dependency, reproducing locally, inspecting code, and checking database/test state.

\section{Probability decision model}
The simulation prior is
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Cause & Prior\\
\midrule
Code regression & 0.40\\
Flaky test & 0.30\\
External dependency & 0.10\\
CI infrastructure & 0.10\\
Test-data/state & 0.05\\
Other & 0.05\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These are model assumptions, not measured population frequencies. Qualitative evidence assumptions are mapped to 0.8 (often), 0.4 (sometimes), and 0.1 (rarely). Bayesian updating makes the decision record explicit and reproducible.

After \texttt{RERUN=PASSED}, the model gives approximately 52.17\% probability to the flaky-test hypothesis and 34.78\% to code regression. After \texttt{RERUN=FAILED}, the corresponding values are approximately 11.11\% and 44.44\%. A rerun changes belief; it does not prove a root cause.

\section{Costs and stopping}
The simulator uses relative diagnostic costs of 1 for cheap automated checks and 2 for heavier diagnostics. Human review/intervention are represented by consequence classes of 8 and 9. These numbers are controlled simulation assumptions. Decision cost is treated as diagnostic cost plus the applicable simulated consequence class. The design also recognizes that information is valuable only when it can change the eventual action.

\section{Policies}
\subsection{P0: fixed baseline}
P0 executes the complete predefined diagnostic sequence without adapting to evidence. It is deliberately conservative and provides the fixed-sequence baseline.

\subsection{P2: threshold policy}
P2 updates beliefs after each observation and commits when the leading hypothesis reaches the configured threshold. The default is 70\%, with sensitivity tests at 60\%, 70\%, and 80\%. The threshold is not claimed to be universally optimal.

\subsection{P3: value of information proxy}
P3 chooses the unused diagnostic action with maximum expected information gain per unit diagnostic cost. This is a tractable proxy for decision value, not a full expected-utility calculation. Value of perfect information (VPI) and value of sample information (VSI) are used as extensions to explain why raw entropy reduction is not identical to decision value.

\section{Experiment}
The executable experiment creates 120 cases for each fixed seed and pre-generates the potential observation for every action. All policies are evaluated on the same cases, preventing policies from receiving different hidden worlds merely because they consume different numbers of random draws. Seeds 2026--2030 are used for multi-seed analysis.

\section{Results}
At the 70\% threshold, the verified experiment produced:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Policy & Accuracy & Diagnostic cost & Decision cost & Actions\\
\midrule
P0 & 75.83\% & 9.00 & 14.25 & 6.00\\
P2 & 71.00\% & 5.83 & 10.75 & 4.28\\
P3 & 73.17\% & 5.36 & 10.63 & 3.64\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Relative to P0, P3 reduces mean diagnostic cost by approximately 40.4\% and diagnostic actions by approximately 39.3\%, with a 2.67 percentage-point reduction in accuracy. P2 is cheaper than P0 but has lower accuracy than P3 at this threshold.

Sensitivity analysis shows that at 60\% P3 has 72.50\% accuracy versus P2's 66.17\%; at 70\% P3 has 73.17\% versus 71.00\%; at 80\% P2 has 75.17\% versus P3's 74.50\%. Thus P3 is not universally superior: it provides a strong efficiency/accuracy trade-off under the current assumptions, while P2 can match or exceed it on accuracy at a higher threshold.

\section{Failure analysis}
The verified run produced 1,800 policy-case records and five representative incorrect decisions were examined. Examples include P3 misclassifying a simulated flaky test as test-data/state, P2 confusing test-data/state with an external dependency, and P0 confusing CI infrastructure or an unknown cause with code regression. The maximum observed decision cost was 17, tied across multiple incorrect cases. The detailed case records and observations are maintained in \texttt{results/final\_results.md} and the error-analysis document.

\section{Extensions}
Two extensions beyond the supplied information-theory ladder are used: value of perfect information (VPI) and value of sample information (VSI). VPI provides an upper bound on the value of learning the true state, while VSI focuses on the expected decision improvement from a particular imperfect observation. These concepts expose a limitation of pure EIG: information can reduce entropy without changing the eventual action.

\section{Limitations}
The simulated priors and likelihoods are not calibrated to a production CI dataset. The six causes may be incomplete. Historical failure comparability is not validated. A single passing rerun does not establish flakiness. Community discussion is treated as practitioner evidence, not scientific ground truth. The experiment therefore supports claims about the behavior of the simulated policies under stated assumptions, not general claims about real CI systems or production ROI.
